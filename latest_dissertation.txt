Abstract
This dissertation studies whether tokenized gold is merely a liquid digital version of gold or
whether it also contains useful price information when traditional gold markets are less active.
The analysis focuses on PAX Gold (PAXG) and Tether Gold (XAUT), which are physically
backed tokenized gold assets traded continuously. The study first tests whether tokenized gold
is linked to traditional gold in the long run. Using cointegration methods, the results show that
both tokens remain closely tied to gold prices. The dissertation then examines weekday price
discovery using a VECM and Gonzalo–Granger shares. Against spot gold, the token can
appear to lead, but against gold futures it tends to follow.
This shows that weekday leadership is not robust and is sensitive to benchmark choice and
closing-time differences. The core analysis then turns to weekends. The results show that
weekend token returns help predict the subsequent Monday adjustment in gold prices. This
relationship remains visible in a cleaner hourly pre-reopen design and survives out-of-sample
testing, although it is stronger and more stable for XAUT than for PAXG. Additional placebo,
interaction, and decay tests show that the predictive relationship is materially stronger during
the weekend closure window and is absorbed rapidly once the traditional market reopens.
Weekend volatility also helps explain the size of the following Monday move, with stronger
evidence for XAUT.
Overall, the dissertation finds evidence consistent with tokenized gold containing useful offhours information when traditional gold trading is limited. At the same time, the results show
that measured price discovery is sensitive to benchmark choice, timing conventions, and
market stress, which should be treated cautiously in future research.

1. Introduction
1.1. Tokenization and the question of informative liquidity
In recent years, asset tokenization has attracted a great deal of attention. In May 2025
the World Economic Forum (WEF) devoted a report to the subject, Asset Tokenization in
Financial Markets: The Next Generation of Value Exchange, arguing that adoption of
asset tokenization is being driven by market demand for speed, efficiency and
connectivity (WEF, 2025). Tokenization is defined as the process of generating and
recording a digital representation of traditional assets on a programmable platform (BIS,
2024). As a result, traditional assets such as treasury debt, commodities, asset-backed
credit, specialty finance, equities, and real estate can be represented in digital form on
blockchain networks. The main attraction of tokenization is the promise of continuous
trading, which provides liquidity and always allows access to assets, unlike assets that
are subject to the closing of traditional markets.
Yet a market that is open is not necessarily a market that is informative. Off-hours crypto
trading is often characterised as thinner and more retail-driven than institutional markets
(Mafrur, 2026). The descriptive statistics given in Section 4.3 show that the volume of
token trading on weekends is less than that on weekdays. Trading activity alone doesn’t
guarantee informative prices; it’s an empirical question whether these prices convey
information, which has not been directly investigated.
This study examines price discovery in tokenized gold. Gold has long been regarded as
a leading safe-haven asset because of its scarcity and its ability to hedge against broad
economic uncertainty, but holding physical gold involves storage, insurance, and liquidity
constraints. Since 2019, tokenized gold has emerged as a bridge between traditional
markets and decentralized finance, with products such as PAX Gold (PAXG) and Tether
Gold (XAUT) offering 1:1 physically backed digital claims that trade continuously with
relatively low friction (Ashfaq et al., 2026). The segment has also grown rapidly: tokenized
commodities market capitalisation rose by 289% between the start of 2025 and the end
of Q1 2026, from $1.43 billion to $5.55 billion, while tokenized gold spot trading volume
reached $90.7 billion in Q1 2026 alone, exceeding the $84.6 billion traded during all of
2025 (CoinGecko, 2026).
Gold offers an ideal setting to assess the informativeness of tokenized liquidity. (Mafrur,
2026) notes that gold-backed tokens have larger holder bases and more persistent onchain activity than other real-world assets, making them the most suitable for determining

6

the information content of tokenized trading. Additionally, gold’s established reference
market, available in both spot and futures, allows for accurate benchmarking of token
prices against market prices, unlike most tokenized assets. Since these tokens are
backed one-for-one by physical gold, their long-term relationship with the underlying
asset is mechanically close, making short-term deviations informational rather than
reflecting fundamental differences.
Continuous trading is crucial, especially on weekends. Traditional gold benchmarks don’t
trade 24/7 and face market closures or limited trading windows. This creates an
information gap: news during these periods can affect token prices but only impact gold
benchmarks when trading resumes. Thus, we must question if weekend changes in token
prices genuinely reflect gold’s value, which becomes visible in the traditional market when
it reopens.

1.2. Institutional background: Tokenized versus Traditional Gold
PAXG and XAUT are Ethereum-based ERC-20 tokens representing one fine troy ounce
of a serialized London Good Delivery gold bar, redeemable for the actual metal when a
threshold is met (Paxos, 2019; TG Commodities, 2022). PAXG is issued by Paxos, a trust
company in New York regulated by the NYDFS, with gold held in London. XAUT is issued
by TG Commodities, associated with Tether, with no financial regulatory oversight and
gold stored in Switzerland. Due to these differences, their regulatory status and custody
arrangements warrant separate examination. Minted in equal quantities to the gold bars,
arbitrage mechanisms ensure token prices closely align with gold prices, providing the
institutional foundation for the long-run linkage in Chapter 4. Unlike OTC gold, ETFs, and
COMEX futures, which close over the weekend, these tokens are traded continuously,
which makes tokenized gold the only market in which gold can be traded during the
weekend. Table 1 summarises this comparison.

1.3. Gap and Research Questions
Despite the rapid growth of these instruments, an important research gap remains.
Existing studies on tokenized gold have focused mainly on tracking accuracy relative to
spot gold, showing that token prices move closely with the underlying asset, but not
whether they play an independent role in price formation. Research on the liquidity of
tokenized assets, for example Mafrur, (2026), confirms that trading activity exists but does
not test whether that trading is informative. As a result, current work still says little about
whether tokenized gold contains useful price information, especially when traditional gold
trading is limited.
The broader price-discovery literature has long examined leadership across linked spot
and futures markets. Gold-specific studies provide the most relevant benchmark here.
Hauptfleisch, Putniņš and Lucey, (2016) show that New York gold futures play a larger
role on average than the London spot market in setting the gold price. Nath et al., (2019)
find a long-run cointegrating relationship between Indian gold futures and spot prices and
report that daily price discovery takes place primarily in the futures market. Sobti, Sehgal,
and Sehgal, Sobti and Diesting, (2021) likewise show that futures often play an important
role in intraday gold price discovery, although leadership varies across instruments and
market settings. The emergence of tokenized gold creates a new setting in which the
asset can trade continuously, raising a question not addressed by this earlier literature:
how does tokenized gold compare with established gold benchmarks, and does it contain
informative price signals or mainly follow them? Because tokenized gold trades
continuously while traditional gold benchmarks do not, this comparison is especially
important during periods when the reference market is closed or less able to adjust
immediately.
This dissertation addresses that gap through three main questions. First, when the
conventional gold market is open, does tokenized gold lead or follow in the pricediscovery process? Second, do weekend changes in token prices help predict how gold
adjusts when traditional trading resumes, and is this relationship materially stronger
during the closure window than during ordinary weekday trading? Third, does the token
contain information about the size of the upcoming adjustment, and not only its direction?
To answer these questions, the study constructs a daily dataset for the two main goldbacked tokens, PAX Gold (PAXG) and Tether Gold (XAUT), alongside spot gold and
COMEX gold futures from 2019 to 2026. Weekday price discovery is examined using a
vector error-correction model (VECM) with Gonzalo-Granger component shares.
Weekend-event regressions are used to study information transmission outside regular

8

trading hours, with heteroskedasticity- and autocorrelation-consistent (HAC) standard
errors. These tests are complemented by placebo, interaction, and decay checks to
assess whether the observed weekend effect is stronger when the traditional market is
closed and whether it disappears once trading resumes. The analysis is limited to daily
data and to the two dominant gold-backed tokens.

1.4. Contributions
The dissertation offers three main contributions. First, it reorients the empirical study of
tokenised assets from a focus on liquidity alone to the informativeness of that liquidity,
examining tokenised gold through the lens of price discovery rather than relying only on
activity-based indicators such as turnover, number of holders, or trading persistence.
Second, it provides evidence consistent with tokenised gold containing off-hours
predictive information when traditional gold trading is limited: weekend movements in the
token help predict how gold adjusts when trading resumes, both in direction and in
magnitude, whereas weekday results do not show robust independent token leadership
once benchmark sensitivity is taken seriously. The evidence further suggests that this
information is absorbed rapidly at reopening, since no Monday intraday trading advantage
is identified. Third, the dissertation develops an identification strategy suited to this
setting, combining placebo, interaction, and decay tests to distinguish the weekend effect
from a more general lead-lag relationship, while also showing that measured price
discovery is materially affected by benchmark choice and by non-synchronous closing
times.

1.5. Dissertation Structure
The remainder of this dissertation is structured as follows. Chapter 2 reviews the relevant
literature, including the concept of tokenization and real-world assets, the institutional
structure of tokenized gold, cointegration and long-run linkage, price discovery, and offhours information transmission, and situates the study within this body of work. Chapter
3 outlines the empirical strategy, detailing the methods used to test each hypothesis.
Chapter 4 describes the data employed in the study, including sources, sample
construction, variable definitions, summary statistics, and stationarity checks. Chapter 5
presents the results in the order of the hypotheses, covering long-run linkage, weekday
price discovery, weekend prediction, and weekend volatility transmission, along with
identification and efficiency checks that show the effect is stronger during the closure
window and demonstrate that the information is incorporated at the reopening rather than
being tradeable. Chapter 6 concludes and suggests directions for future research.

2. Literature Review
2.1. Tokenization and price linkage
Existing research on tokenized gold shows strong long-run alignment with gold prices,
but it mainly stops at tracking accuracy, correlation, volatility, and design differences
rather than testing whether the token contributes to price formation when the reference
market is closed (Ashfaq et al., 2026b). More broadly, the spillover literature shows that
crypto markets can transmit shocks to traditional financial markets, including gold,
especially during stress periods. However, that evidence is usually estimated at the assetclass level rather than at the level of a specific token and its own underlying commodity,
so it does not isolate whether a gold-backed token itself contributes to spot-gold price
formation (Iyer and Popescu, 2023) (Bawa, 2026).
Carapella et al. (2023) argue that tokenization can strengthen links between the crypto
ecosystem and traditional financial markets. At a sufficient scale, tokenized assets may
transmit volatility or information from crypto trading into the markets for their underlying
assets. This possibility lies at the heart of the present study. If information can move from
the token to the underlying asset, then the token is not merely a passive reflection of gold
but may contribute to price formation. Whether it plays that role is an empirical question,
and the question becomes especially relevant when the reference market is closed.
Research on tokenization outside gold also helps explain why a price link should exist.
Across RWA studies, tokens are treated as on-chain representations whose value
depends on legal structure, custody, servicing, verification, and redemption rather than
on transferability alone (Vella, Pennella and Ballandies, 2026). Empirical RWA evidence
also shows that token prices can track underlying fundamentals while still being distorted
by thin trading and crypto-specific forces. For example, real-estate token studies show
broad co-movement with housing-price indices, suggesting real economic exposure, but
also higher volatility than the underlying asset itself (Swinkels, 2023). This is directly
relevant to tokenized gold: even when the reference asset anchors value, market
microstructure can still create deviations around that anchor (Ashfaq et al., 2026).
Tokenized gold is therefore best understood as a hybrid instrument: structurally anchored
to spot gold through custody, reserve verification, valuation, and redemption, but traded
in a 24/7 crypto environment that can generate short-run deviations and potentially
transmit information back to traditional markets (Vella, Pennella and Ballandies, 2026);
(Ashfaq et al., 2026b). The unresolved question is not whether a price link exists, but
whether that link is strong enough for weekend token trading to contribute to Monday gold
price discovery, which is exactly the empirical gap your study addresses

2.2. Tokenized Gold Prior Research
Prior research on tokenized gold goes beyond simple price tracking and also examines
hedging, safe-haven properties, spillovers, and market microstructure. However, none of
these studies directly tests whether weekend token trading contributes to the subsequent
adjustment in gold prices when the traditional market is closed (Ashfaq et al., 2026; Jalan,
Matkovskyy and Yarovaya, 2021; Loukil et al., 2025).
Ashfaq et al. (2026) examine whether PAXG and XAUT accurately replicate the price
behaviour of conventional gold. They find that both tokens maintain a strong long-run
relationship with gold, although short-run discrepancies can arise because of continuous
crypto trading, fragmented liquidity, and issuer-specific design features. Their results also
suggest that XAUT is more closely aligned with spot gold than PAXG. This study is
important because it establishes that tokenized gold is structurally linked to the underlying
metal, but it stops at tracking quality rather than asking whether the token contributes to
price formation.
Jalan, Matkovskyy and Yarovaya, (2021) study whether gold-backed cryptocurrencies
retain gold’s safe-haven properties during the COVID-19 period. Their evidence suggests
that these assets received volatility transmitted from gold markets while also carrying
risks comparable to those of broader crypto assets, which weakens the case that they
function as safe havens in the same way as physical gold. This result is relevant because
it shows that gold backing alone does not eliminate crypto-market influences from token
pricing.
Harvey et al. (2026) ask a broader question about whether tokenized gold can restore
some of the functions of gold in a digital setting. Like Ashfaq et al. (2026), they confirm
that gold-backed tokens closely track spot gold, ETFs, and futures prices. Their
contribution is to show that this alignment remains strong even during macroeconomic
announcements, geopolitical shocks, and crypto-specific stress events. Even so, their
evidence is largely case-based and does not provide a systematic test of whether
tokenized gold generates independent off-hours price information.
Taken together, these studies show that tokenized gold is a close and generally reliable
representation of the underlying asset, and that this link often remains strong even during
turbulent periods. But tracking and price discovery are not the same thing. An asset can
follow gold very closely without contributing any new information of its own. That
distinction becomes most important when the traditional market is closed. The present
dissertation addresses that gap by moving beyond tracking accuracy and testing whether
tokenized gold contributes to price discovery during regular trading hours and whether its

11

weekend price movements help predict the subsequent adjustment in gold prices when
traditional trading resumes.

2.3. Cointegration and Long-Run Linkage
Gold prices are not stationary since they fluctuate over time without settling down to any
level. If an ordinary regression is carried out between two non-stationary series, a false
relationship may result, one that looks statistically strong but reflects no real relationship.
Cointegration avoids this: if the gap between the token price and the gold price stays
stable over time even though each series drifts on its own, the two are cointegrated, and
that stability is what justifies treating them as genuinely linked rather than merely
correlated. Engle and Granger (1987) developed this idea by introducing the concepts of
cointegration and error correction, along with a two-step method for estimating the longrun relationship between the linked price series. Using this framework enables the study
to test whether PAXG and XAUT share a stable long-run equilibrium with gold, which
forms the basis for the price-discovery analysis (this is tested as hypothesis H1 in Chapter
4).
Ashfaq et al. (2026) provide the closest empirical precedent, confirming this long-run
alignment for PAXG and XAUT over multi-year data. Their analysis, however, stops at
tracking accuracy: it does not address which market leads when the two prices diverge.
This study therefore takes their result as the baseline linkage to re-confirm (H1) and
extends it toward price discovery.
The reason why one would expect there to be a link is given by Lechner (2026), who
argues that the spot price of the underlying commodity is the only appropriate reference
price for a tokenized commodity, no matter what the issuer's cost of acquisition or its
reserve arrangement is. Based on the Law of One Price and the empirical evidence
concerning physically backed gold instruments, Lechner shows that arbitrage and the
ability to redeem the token tie it to the spot price, any deviations from this being due to
fees and arbitrage frictions and not to fundamental factors. It is for this reason that spot
gold is chosen as the benchmark for the token's long-run equilibrium. This theoretical
rationale is not directly tested on these instruments. Instead, the cointegration test in
Chapter 4 empirically establishes whether PAXG and XAUT’s prices behave as Lechner’s
arbitrage mechanism predicts.

2.4. Price Discovery
Price discovery involves incorporating information into related asset prices. In linked
markets, the central question is not only price movement, but also which market adjusts
when temporary deviations occur. Since tokenized gold and traditional gold are expected

12

to share a long-run equilibrium relationship, the relevant issue is not just their comovement, but which side adjusts when their prices diverge to restore equilibrium.
To address this question, the study applies a Vector Error Correction Model (VECM),
which combines long-run equilibrium with short-run adjustment dynamics in a single
framework (Engle & Granger, 1987; Chen, 2022). The error-correction coefficients
indicate how strongly each market responds to disequilibrium and therefore provide
evidence on which side leads the adjustment process. Gonzalo and Granger’s (1995)
decomposition then transforms this structure into interpretable price discovery share,
showing the portion of the common price movement that comes from the token side and
the portion that comes from gold.
The two measures that are most prominent in the empirical literature on price discovery
are Hasbrouck’s (1995) information share and the Gonzalo–Granger (1995) component
share. Hasbrouck’s measure is best suited to high-frequency settings, where nearsimultaneous information arrival can be identified more precisely. Does
The broader price-discovery literature has long emphasized the role of futures markets in
incorporating information rapidly (Garbade and Silber, 1983). However, evidence from
other asset classes such as Bitcoin, equity-index, or NIFTY spot-futures markets should
be treated primarily as support for the methodology rather than as direct evidence on gold
itself. Gold-specific studies provide a more relevant benchmark. (Hauptfleisch, Putniņš
and Lucey, 2016) show that New York gold futures play a larger role on average than the
London spot market in setting the gold price. Nath et al., (2019) report a long-run
cointegrating relationship between Indian gold futures and spot prices and find that daily
price discovery occurs primarily in the futures market. Sobti, Sehgal and Diesting, (2021)
likewise show that futures are often an important leader in intraday gold price discovery,
although leadership varies across instruments and market settings. These studies
support the use of gold futures as a meaningful benchmark in the present analysis, while
also suggesting that price-discovery results may depend on market structure and timing
conventions. That possibility is especially relevant here, because the measured
leadership of tokenized gold may vary with benchmark choice and with non-synchronous
closing times.

2.5. OL-hours Information and Weekend Prediction
Prior research shows that after-hours and overnight trading can contain real information
rather than mere noise, and that this information can affect the next regular-session
opening price (Barclay and Hendershott, 2003; Chiu et al., 2024) Although trading outside

13

regular hours is often thinner and less efficient than daytime trading, it can still contribute
to price discovery.
When related assets trade on different clocks, price discovery may shift into the nonoverlapping trading window rather than occurring only during the regular session. Clear
evidence comes from ADR markets and from partially overlapping ETF-futures markets,
where prices formed in the market that remains open are subsequently reflected in the
market that reopens later (Hoang and Vo, 2026). n gold markets specifically, futures often
lead spot and ETFs in intraday price discovery, although that leadership depends on
liquidity, trading overlap, and market conditions (Sehgal, Sobti and Diesting, 2021).
This is the core intuition behind the weekend analysis in the present dissertation.
Tokenized gold trades continuously, whereas the main traditional gold benchmarks do not
share the same trading availability. COMEX gold futures pause over the weekend and
reopen on Sunday evening, while spot gold has no single exchange close. This creates
a useful setting for testing whether weekend token-price movements contain information
about the subsequent adjustment in gold prices once traditional trading resumes.
This broad idea is also consistent with the wider price-discovery literature, which shows
that differences in market structure, liquidity, and trading hours affect which venue
incorporates information first. In the tokenized-gold setting, Harvey et al., (2026) provide
case-based examples in which tokenized gold appeared to reflect weekend geopolitical
news before traditional benchmarks reopened. At the same time, Ashfaq et al. (2026)
show that tokenized gold tracks the underlying metal closely, while Mafrur (2026) shows
more generally that active token trading does not, by itself, prove that such trading is
informative. Taken together, these studies suggest that weekend token prices may
contain useful information, but they do not establish this systematically.
The unresolved question, therefore, is whether weekend movements in tokenized gold
consistently predict the reopening adjustment in traditional gold prices, and whether that
relationship is materially stronger during the closure window than during ordinary
weekday trading. This is the gap addressed in the present dissertation. Rather than
relying on isolated episodes, the analysis tests the weekend relationship across the full
sample and then applies placebo and decay checks to examine whether the effect is
genuinely linked to the period in which the traditional market is unavailable or limited.

3. Methodology
3.1. Research design and positioning
This dissertation is a quantitative time-series study that tests whether the liquidity
provided by tokenized gold is informative rather than merely present. The design is
econometric and observational, and it relies on a closure-based institutional timing
design: the weekend closure of the traditional gold market creates a recurring window in
which the token trades while the reference market is limited.
All models are built in ordinary least squares (OLS) and extended for financial data.
Cointegration methods are used for non-stationary price levels, and this study uses
Heteroskedasticity and Autocorrelation Consistent (HAC) standard errors, following
Newey and West, (1987) HAC are used throughout to ensure valid inference given daily
returns’ volatility clustering and serial correlation. This conservative choice accounts for
the error process’s precise form by widening standard errors.
This positions the study against the existing literature: Ashfaq et al. (2026) establish that
tokenized gold tracks gold, and Mafrur (2026) shows that tokenized assets can trade
actively without that activity being informative. Neither asks whether the prices the token
produces contribute to price formation. This chapter sets out the data and the empirical
strategy through which that question is answered.
The analysis is organized around four related questions. First, do tokenized gold and
traditional gold share a stable long-run linkage (H1)? Second, when the gold market is
open, does the token lead price discovery or merely follow it (H2)? Third, when the gold
market is closed, does the token predict the direction of the reopening move (H3)? Fourth,
does it also predict the size of that move (H4)? Each hypothesis is matched to a method
chosen for that specific question, summarized in Table 2.

3.2. H1. Long-run linkage
The first hypothesis asks if the token and gold share a genuine long-run equilibrium, which
is crucial for the following analysis. Engle and Granger, (1987) two-step procedure is
used. Since the price levels are non-stationary while the returns are stationary
(established in Chapter 4), an ordinary regression on the levels would risk a spurious
result. Cointegration rules this out. Two series are cointegrated when a linear combination
of them is stationary, so testing for cointegration confirms the realness of the token–gold

3.6. ELiciency, robustness and Software
The study asks how quickly weekend information is incorporated once traditional gold
trading resumes. Monday’s move is separated into the reopening gap (Friday close to
Monday open) and the following session (Monday open to close). If the predictable
component is almost in the reopening gap, the information is impounded at the reopen
and no reliable Monday intraday trading edge is identified. This distinction separates a
price-discovery from investment recommendation.
Several robustness checks accompany the main results. Momentum specifications add
pre-weekend token trends over five and twenty days to test whether the weekend signal
simply reflects continuation. An asymmetry specification tests whether negative weekend
moves predict more strongly than positive ones. Liquidity-conditioning specifications use
trend-adjusted volume measures so that the large growth in token trading activity over
time does not mechanically drive the results. The XAUT sample is also re-estimated over
its more liquid recent period, and the core weekend regression is re-estimated against
gold futures as an alternative benchmark.
All reported t-statistics use HAC (Newey-West) standard errors, so that implication is
robust to the heteroskedasticity and autocorrelation characteristic of financial returns; the
five% significance threshold corresponds to an absolute t-statistic above 1.96. All data
collection, processing, and estimation are implemented in Python, and the full pipeline is
scripted and reproducible from the raw data.

4. Data
4.1. Data Collection
The tokenized gold assets used in this study are PAXG and XAUT, and the daily price
data for these assets are obtained from Yahoo Finance. Two gold benchmarks are used.
Spot gold (XAU/USD), obtained from Alpha Vantage, acts as the conceptual benchmark
because the tokens are one-for-one backed claims on physical gold; it is used for the
long-run linkage analysis and for the core weekend analysis. The GC=F series is the
Yahoo Finance continuous COMEX futures series. Because Yahoo does not fully
document its contract-roll convention, roll stitching may introduce small mechanical
effects into the futures price relationship and is therefore treated as a limitation of the
futures benchmark. Futures nevertheless provide a cleaner exchange-timed reference
than spot gold and are therefore used for the weekday price-discovery tests and the
reopening-gap robustness checks.
The sample begins at each token’s launch date and runs through July 2026. The PAXG
data start in September 2019 and comprise 2,473 daily observations, whereas the XAUT
data begin in February 2020 and include 2,339 daily observations. The gold futures series
begins in January 2018, before the launch of either token, so futures-based tests can
span the full history of both tokens. The weekend-event analysis produces 353 usable
weekends for PAXG and 333 for XAUT.
To address the timing-alignment issue, an hourly timing-robustness sample is constructed
using Yahoo Finance 60-minute data for PAXG-USD, XAUT-USD, and GC=F. All
timestamps are handled in UTC. The COMEX weekly reopening reference is defined as
Sunday 17:00 America/Chicago and then converted to UTC, so daylight-saving changes
are handled automatically. Market holidays and early closures are not adjusted manually:
the construction uses the last available bar at or before the Friday anchor and the first
available bar at or after the Sunday reopening cutoff, and weekends with missing anchor
observations are excluded rather than filled forward. For each Monday event, the
weekend token return is measured from the Friday token anchor to the last token
observation at or before the reopening cutoff, while the futures reopening gap is
measured from the Friday futures anchor to the first GC=F observation at or after that
cutoff. In the sample, this typically corresponds to a Friday 23:00 UTC token anchor, a
Sunday 21:00 UTC final pre-reopening token observation, a Friday 20:00 UTC futures
anchor, and a Sunday 22:00 UTC first futures reopening bar. The hourly sample runs from
19 August 2024 to 27 July 2026 and contains 101 weekend observations.

4.5. Data Splitting
Most tests are estimated on the full sample. The main exception is the out-of-sample
validation of the core weekend result (H3), which uses a one-step-ahead expandingwindow design. The initial training sample consists of 104 weekends. For each
subsequent weekend, the model is estimated using prior weekends only and is then used
to forecast the following unseen weekend; the estimation window expands recursively
over time, and the model is re-estimated each weekend. This procedure ensures that
reported directional accuracy and forecast error reflect genuine out-of-sample
performance rather than in-sample fit, and that each forecast uses only information
available at the forecast date.

for rapid incorporation at the market reopen.

6. Conclusion
6.1. Review
This study asked whether tokenized gold’s continuous liquidity is informative rather than
merely present, testing the question through price discovery in PAXG and XAUT against
spot gold and gold futures from 2019 to 2026. Three questions organised the analysis.
First, when the traditional gold market is open, does the token lead or follow price
discovery? Second, when traditional gold trading is limited over the weekend, do token
movements help predict the subsequent Monday gold-market adjustment? Third, does
tokenized gold contain information not only about the direction of that adjustment but also
about the size? Chapter 5 addressed each of these questions in turn; this chapter brings
the answers together and explains their implications.

6.2. Main Conclusion
The answer to the first question is that the token does not show a robust independent
weekday price leadership. Against spot gold, the token can appear to lead, but against
gold futures it tends to follow. A result that reverses with the benchmark is not a strong
basis for claiming leadership. The most plausible explanation is timing rather than
economics: the token is snapshotted near midnight UTC, spot gold carries no exact
closing timestamp, and futures settle on a New York basis, so a later token observation
can look like it leads a less precisely timed series without possessing any informational
advantage. Read against gold-specific studies that often find an important role for futures
in gold price discovery, the more cautious interpretation is that a small, one-for-onebacked token follows the dominant price-discovery venue when that venue is open
(Hauptfleisch, Putniņš and Lucey, 2016; Nath et al., 2019; Sehgal, Sobti and Diesting,
2021).
That contrast is what makes the weekend result meaningful. When traditional gold trading
is limited, weekend token movements help predict the subsequent Monday adjustment in
gold prices. This appears in direction for both PAXG and XAUT, and in magnitude more
clearly for XAUT than for PAXG. The hourly pre-reopen design supports the same
directional conclusion in a cleaner timing setting, and sensitivity checks show that the
effect is amplified by stress periods but not fully driven by them.
This means that tokenized gold’s informational role is not constant across all settings. It
is most visible when the reference market is closed or less able to adjust immediately,
and it weakens once that market reopens. Read together with the Monday intraday
efficiency check, the evidence is more consistent with rapid information impounding at

37

reopening than with a reliable post-reopen trading opportunity. In that sense, the main
contribution of the dissertation is not to show that tokenized gold always leads gold, but
that it appears to incorporate useful information when traditional gold trading is limited.
This places the dissertation alongside three strands of prior research. Ashfaq et al. (2026)
show that tokenized gold tracks the underlying metal; this study confirms that long-run
linkage but goes further by asking whether the token also contributes information about
subsequent price adjustment. Gold-specific price-discovery studies (Hauptfleisch,
Putniņš and Lucey, 2016; Nath et al., 2019; Sehgal, Sobti and Diesting, 2021) show that
futures often play an important role in gold price discovery relative to spot markets; this
dissertation extends that discussion to continuously traded tokenized gold. At the same
time, the evidence here is bounded by benchmark choice, timing conventions, and the
limits of daily data, all of which should be treated carefully in future work.

6.3. Future Work
One clear next step is the use of richer intraday data. The hourly weekend design already
improves the timing of the reopening test, but the weekday price-discovery result still
depends on daily benchmark conventions. More complete intraday token and futures data
would allow the timing issue to be tested directly rather than inferred.
A second extension is to broaden the cross-section of assets. This study focuses on the
two dominant gold-backed tokens. Applying the same framework to other tokenized
commodities, or to tokenized assets without one-to-one physical backing, would help
show whether off-hours predictive informativeness is specific to tokenized gold or a
broader feature of continuously traded tokenized assets.
A third direction concerns methodology. One of the clearest lessons of the dissertation is
that price-discovery estimates can be sensitive to benchmark choice and nonsynchronous closes. As continuously traded instruments become more common, more
formal treatment of this timing problem will become increasingly important for the wider
price-discovery literature.

Appendix C shows that the formal forecast comparisons are consistent with the descriptive
benchmark ranking, but not equally strongly for every comparator. The evidence is clearest
against the nested basis-only and weekend-return-only models, where Clark-West p-values
are below 0.05 for both PAXG and XAUT. Against the historical-mean and zero benchmarks,
the p-values are borderline, especially for XAUT. The out-of-sample evidence should therefore
be read as strongest against parsimonious predictive alternatives rather than as proof of
uniform forecast dominance.
